    How voice answers work
  • Say the English meaning, or the Japanese reading, when the bar asks for it. You can also type (romaji turns into kana).
  • Say "I don't know" (or わかりません) to see the answer.
  • After a wrong answer, say "next" to count it, or "again" if it misheard you. Nothing counts as wrong until then. Press p or 🔊 to hear the reading, and m or ▶ to hear what the mic recorded: if that sounds right, it was the speech-to-text.
  • Short readings starting with r (りょう, りゅう) are hard for speech-to-text on their own. Say them twice (りょう、りょう) or as 読みはりょう.
  • With "let me check the results" on, nothing goes to WaniKani until you've looked over the list at the end. You can mark a miss as correct there, or untick an item to leave it due.
  • Vowel length is forgiven (speech-to-text often hears がっこう as がっこ). A near miss, like じ for し, asks you to say it again.
  • Sending results needs a token with the reviews:create permission (WaniKani → Settings → API Tokens). Unsent results wait here and resend.
  • Speech-to-text runs on KBU, so voice answers need that PC on.

    VOICEVOX setup
  • Install VOICEVOX from voicevox.hiroshiba.jp and start it — its engine listens on localhost:50021.
  • Serve this page from localhost so the engine accepts requests: run python3 -m http.server 8000 in the folder with this file, then open http://localhost:8000/wanikani_listening_practice.html.
  • If Connect fails from a file:// page, open the engine's settings at 127.0.0.1:50021/setting and set CORS Policy Mode to all (or launch the engine with --cors_policy_mode all).
  • Click Connect, pick a speaker, then Test voice.

    • Voice quality depends on your browser/OS. Microsoft Edge has excellent natural Japanese voices; Chrome's "Google 日本語" is good too. On macOS you can add voices in System Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content.
